## Design Context

### Users
Power users who run Zen Browser on 2–3 machines (home, work, laptop). Tech-savvy, likely designers or developers who care about workflow details. They open Zync purposefully when switching between machines — it's a task-oriented interaction, not casual browsing. They have opinions about their tools and notice when something is well-made.

### Brand Personality
**Playful, Opinionated, Alive.** Zync has a point of view — it's not a generic utility. It feels made by someone who cares about every detail. Confident without being arrogant. The kind of app you'd recommend to a friend and feel slightly smug about using.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme**: Light — warm cream base (oklch ~96.5% lightness, 88° hue), coral/orange-red accent, charcoal text
- **Fonts**: Anybody (variable width+weight) for distinctive condensed UI labels; Red Hat Mono for technical elements (sync codes, countdowns)
- **Embellishment**: Distinctive & bold — physical button depth shadows, animated SVG check drawing, spring-animated sliding tab indicator, subtle dot-grid background texture
- **Color philosophy**: Coral is the ONLY accent — used only for primary CTAs, brand icon, and success/accent states. Never decorative. High contrast through rarity.

### Design Principles
1. **Every interaction should feel satisfying** — buttons have physical depth, clicks respond tactilely, sync codes are displayed as mini artifacts (ticket aesthetic with dashed border)
2. **Typography does the heavy lifting** — weight contrast (300→800) creates hierarchy without relying on color
3. **The sync code moment is the hero** — receiving a code should feel special, like a boarding pass
4. **Dense but not cramped** — 420×460px is tight. Every pixel earns its place
5. **Coral is precious** — its rarity is its power
