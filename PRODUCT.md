# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Any Zen Browser user who discovered ZynC on their own — not necessarily technical. They use Zen on two or more machines and want their workspaces, pinned tabs, and themes to carry over without thinking about it. Their headspace when opening ZynC: mildly hopeful, vaguely worried about messing something up. They are not power-users of sync tools; they just want it to work.

## Product Purpose

ZynC moves Zen Browser profile data (workspaces, tabs, themes, extensions) between machines using a one-time encrypted sync code. No account, no server, no ongoing config. The user uploads from one machine, enters the code on another, done. Success looks like: open Zen on the second machine and nothing is missing.

## Brand Personality

Friendly, clear, a little delightful. Feels like a well-loved indie Mac app — considered without being precious. The warmth comes from the typography and palette, not from mascots or clever copy.

## Anti-references

- Developer tooling (terminal aesthetic, dense monospace, dark-first) — wrong register for this audience
- Generic SaaS (white background, blue primaries, card grids) — interchangeable, nothing distinctive
- Overwrought macOS design (translucency, vibrancy, excessive chrome) — the cream + Bricolage direction deliberately avoids this
- Cluttered UIs — too many options, too many decisions. ZynC has two actions; that's the whole product.

## Design Principles

1. **Zero anxiety.** The user is syncing something they care about. Every state — loading, success, error — should feel safe and legible. No ambiguity about what just happened.
2. **Earn every pixel.** The window is 420×460px. Nothing renders that doesn't serve the current action. Decorative elements require justification.
3. **Actions over explanations.** Labels and affordances should be self-evident. Instructions mean the UI failed.
4. **Warmth without whimsy.** The cream + orange palette is deliberate, not quirky. Delight comes from precision and small polish moments, not from decoration.
5. **Quiet confidence.** The app knows what it's doing. Copy is direct; feedback is immediate; the visual language is calm.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Standard contrast requirements, keyboard accessibility. No known specific user needs beyond baseline.
